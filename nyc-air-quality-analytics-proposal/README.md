# NYC Air Quality & Public Health Analytics Proposal
## New York City Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH)

## Overview
This project proposes a data analytics initiative for the New York City Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH) to integrate air quality, health, and demographic data in order to predict and mitigate respiratory-related health risks such as asthma.

The proposal focuses on moving from descriptive reporting toward predictive analytics and real-time decision support to improve public health outcomes, reduce hospital admissions, and promote environmental justice across New York City neighborhoods.

---

## Problem Statement
New York City experiences significant variation in air quality across neighborhoods, with underserved communities facing disproportionately high exposure to pollutants such as PM₂.₅ and NO₂. These environmental factors are strongly linked to asthma-related emergency room visits and chronic respiratory illness.

Current reporting tools are largely reactive and historical, limiting DOHMH’s ability to anticipate health crises or target interventions proactively.

---

## Data Sources
- Air quality sensor data (PM₂.₅, NO₂, O₃)
- Hospital and emergency room admission records
- Demographic and census data
- Seasonal and weather data (temperature, humidity)
- Policy and infrastructure indicators (green space, emission zones)

All health data is assumed to be de-identified and handled in compliance with HIPAA and city data governance policies.

---

## Proposed Analytics Initiative
The proposal recommends developing a predictive environmental health dashboard with the following components:

- Predictive models to forecast asthma-related ER visits
- Neighborhood risk scoring to prioritize interventions
- Hotspot mapping to identify pollution-health overlap
- Seasonal trend analysis
- Real-time alerts to schools, clinics, and residents during pollution spikes
- Evaluation framework to measure policy and infrastructure impact

---

## Data Analytics Life Cycle
This initiative follows the standard data analytics life cycle:

1. **Discovery** – Identify pollution-driven respiratory illness as a key public health risk  
2. **Data Preparation** – Clean, validate, and integrate environmental, health, and demographic data  
3. **Model Planning** – Explore statistical and machine learning models  
4. **Model Building** – Develop predictive and risk-scoring models  
5. **Communication** – Deploy dashboards and visualizations for stakeholders  
6. **Operationalization** – Support real-time alerts and policy decision-making
