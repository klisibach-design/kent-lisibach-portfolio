# Is Higher Education Worth the Cost?
## Decision Analysis of Degree ROI Using Power BI

## Overview
This project evaluates whether higher education provides a meaningful long-term financial return on investment (ROI). Using publicly available salary data, I applied decision analysis techniques to examine how undergraduate major influences early-career earnings, mid-career earnings, salary growth, and upside potential.

The analysis was conducted using a top-down decomposition tree in Power BI, allowing transparent exploration of which factors most strongly influence long-term salary outcomes.

---

## Research Question
How does undergraduate major influence long-term financial ROI, as measured by mid-career earnings and salary growth?

---

## Data Sources
- Degrees That Pay Back
- Salaries by Region
- Salaries by College Type

All datasets contain aggregated, non-identifiable salary data.

---

## Methodology
- Data cleaning and validation
- Salary normalization and formatting
- Exploratory distribution analysis
- Top-down decomposition tree modeling in Power BI

**Target Metric**
- Mid-career median salary (proxy for long-term ROI)

**Explain-By Variables**
- Undergraduate major
- Percent salary growth
- Starting median salary
- Mid-career 75th percentile salary
- Mid-career 90th percentile salary

---

## Key Findings
- Engineering majors dominate median mid-career earnings
- Salary growth explains divergence among similar STEM fields
- Majors such as Economics and Physics exhibit high upside potential despite lower starting salaries
- Upper-percentile earnings reveal risk–reward tradeoffs not captured by median salary alone

---

## Ethical Considerations
- Results reflect aggregated trends, not individual guarantees
- Salary outcomes are influenced by structural and socioeconomic factors
- Findings are presented to support informed decision-making
